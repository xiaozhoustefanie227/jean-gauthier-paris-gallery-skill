# Jean Gauthier × Paris Gallery Skill V4

## Positioning

**Private notebook / gallery paper work / poetic abstraction**

This skill transforms an image into a **quiet paper-based artwork** inspired by the visual language associated with Jean Gauthier:
- intimate
- unfinished
- lightly abstract
- collage-touched
- emotionally selective
- spacious

It should not feel like a polished illustration or a commercial poster.

---

## Core Principle

Do **not** fully recreate the source image.

Instead:
- extract the most emotional fragment
- simplify it
- partially abstract it
- let it breathe inside a large paper field

The result should feel like:
- a private artist note
- a paper study
- an unfinished gallery piece
- a collected fragment from an atelier

---

## Composition

- Use warm ivory / off-white / aged paper as background
- Preserve generous negative space: **60%–85%**
- The visual subject should usually occupy **15%–35%**
- Avoid filling the page
- Avoid polished poster symmetry

The work should feel open, quiet, and slightly accidental.

---

## Fragment Logic

Do not render the whole scene.

### Landscapes
Keep only one or two fragments such as:
- a ridge
- a water shape
- a shoreline
- a horizon
- a small human figure
- a bench
- a rock cluster

### Portraits
Keep only the most expressive parts:
- contour of the face
- jawline
- neck
- eye structure
- lip color
- hair mass / loose scribble

### Furniture / Objects
Treat them as memory-fragments:
- outline first
- scribbled interior
- emotional patches of color
- incomplete geometry welcome

Missing parts are encouraged.

---

## Drawing Language

Use:
- delicate graphite line
- thin unstable contour
- broken line work
- repeated sketch marks
- charcoal smudges
- pastel rubs
- light watercolor traces
- slight paper grain

The line should feel:
- hesitant
- human
- revised
- unfinished

---

## Color

### Base
- ivory
- cream
- warm white
- ash paper

### Main muted tones
- charcoal black
- dusty blue
- faded cobalt
- muted red
- pale pink
- grey blue
- ochre
- moss green

### Accent
Use only small jumps of stronger color:
- cobalt
- red
- orange
- yellow
- green

Most of the image should remain restrained.

---

## Collage Elements

Collage is welcome, but only in a sparse, casual way.

Possible elements:
- tiny vintage paper scraps
- small French text fragments
- old book strips
- label pieces
- music note fragments
- archival snippets
- subtle technical/grid pieces

Rules:
- use sparingly
- make them feel incidental
- do not turn the work into a scrapbook
- do not over-design

---

## Typography & Dates

Text should be minimal:
- small typed words
- tiny labels
- short handwritten notes
- French fragments

Examples:
- étude
- souvenir
- atelier
- archive
- speak
- je vais
- une vie
- last three
- Paris

### Date Rule
- By default: **do not include a date**
- If a date is included, it must be **2026 only**
- Never use older years such as 2024

Allowed examples:
- 2026
- 17 MAI 2026
- MMXXVI

---

## Emotional Quality

The result should feel:
- quiet
- poetic
- intimate
- slightly lonely
- elegant
- unfinished
- tactile

---

## Avoid

Avoid:
- polished illustration
- full-scene rendering
- glossy digital painting
- commercial poster design
- excessive decoration
- too much text
- over-structured graphic layout
- too many collage pieces
- perfect realism

---

## Prompt Template

Create an artwork in **Jean Gauthier × Paris Gallery Skill V4** style.

Do not fully recreate the source image.
Extract only the most emotional fragment of the subject and transform it into a quiet paper-based artist notebook piece.

Use a warm ivory paper background with generous negative space.
Keep the subject relatively small and incomplete.

Use delicate graphite lines, charcoal smudges, soft pastel pigments, watercolor traces, rough scribbles, and imperfect hand-drawn contours.
Allow broken outlines, unfinished forms, selective abstraction, and missing parts.

If the subject is a portrait, emphasize mood through minimal facial structure, pale skin-like tones, loose dark hair marks, and small color accents.
If the subject is furniture or an object, treat it as a memory fragment with a light outline, scribbled interior, and emotional color patches.
If the subject is a landscape, reduce it to one or two poetic fragments such as a ridge, water shape, shoreline, horizon, or isolated figure.

Add only a few subtle collage elements:
tiny vintage paper scraps, small French text fragments, archival labels, or handwritten notes.
Keep them sparse and incidental.

Do not include a date by default.
If a date is included, it must be from 2026 only.

Avoid polished illustration, full-scene rendering, commercial poster design, and busy composition.
